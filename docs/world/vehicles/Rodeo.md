# Rodeo

Pickup trucks and 4x4s.

## Manufacturer history and character

Rodeo was founded in the United States in 1965 to build trucks for owners who
worked on rough ground but still had to drive home on pavement. Its early
success came from simple pickups with usable beds, tall suspension, and
mechanicals a rural shop could understand. The name draws on ranch country,
but the brand is not costume western: its customers include farmers,
contractors, utility crews, and people who simply need a vehicle that can take
a bad road without an apology.

In the 1970s, Rodeo made four-wheel drive and long-travel suspension central to
its identity. The company became known for truck frames that outlasted their
first paint and for owner manuals written like practical advice instead of
sales copy. Later family 4x4s widened the audience without changing that
character.

The **Grazer 4x4** is the present expression of Rodeo: broad-shouldered,
work-ready, and happy to look slightly too tough in a grocery-store parking
lot. Its black-and-orange branding-iron R is fitted to the grille and tailgate.
See the [Grazer emblem record](../../assets/rodeo-grazer-emblem.md) for the
implemented badge and evidence.

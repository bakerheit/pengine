# BWC 360

Original compact four-door Belgian sedan, built for the early-PS2 vehicle finish. BWC means Belgium Working Coach. The 360 uses the established silver-edged black roundel, curved BWC lettering, and Belgian-color center from [the brand roster](../design/vehicle-brands.md).

The native model has four independently hinged doors, six separate glass meshes, a five-seat interior, left-side controls, matte touring tires, 16-spoke alloy wheels, and blank front/rear plate brackets. The roundel appears on the hood, trunk, steering wheel and wheel centers. The color atlas is 256×256 RGBA; its badge lettering uses the repository's Roboto font, rasterized during the cook.

The current revision follows the supplied late-1990s sedan references: an arched roof and window line, broad pressed fenders, a swept twin-grille nose with paired round headlamps, amber corners, blue-silver paint, slim black side moldings, and rounded bumper returns. Wheel anchors, left-side controls and the four-door rig are retained. Door skins and adjacent quarters now share one height contour. The rear quarter is cut to the curved rear-door edge, removing the buried overlap. Door edges use a 1.5 mm radius, with 6 mm side shut lines and a 4 mm roof gap backed by recessed seals, including the A/C pillar corners. The four hinges sit at the leading edges so the tighter doors can swing outward. The front uses actual cut grille, headlamp, fog-lamp and air-inlet openings. Connected rounded returns place the chrome rims near the painted surface, with the vanes and optics set behind it. Impact moldings follow the bumper section at a 2 mm offset. The front plate backing is fitted close to the bumper, and the rear uses a curved stamping with inset combined lamp lenses. Its transverse stations preserve the tail curve through export. The rear roundel and 360 script follow the painted surface, with the script clear of the lamp aperture; the plate pocket crosses the upper bumper and has a blank backing inside the body. The rear plate anchor is `(0, 0.650, -2.233)` in runtime axes, with 4 mm clearance ahead of its backing.

## Driving spec

The 360 is a **1,430 kg rear-wheel-drive compact sedan**. Its native dimensions are a 2.57 m wheelbase, 1.51 m track, and 315 mm tire radius. The gameplay tune uses 456 N.m peak engine torque, a 10% stronger service brake, 16% more lateral grip, 8% stiffer springs, 12% more anti-roll support, and a 15 mm lower centre of mass than the base Classic GTA profile. The rear axle uses a strong coupling and a 94% post-limit grip floor, so ordinary throttle stays planted instead of turning into a slide. Steering keeps the model-safe 0.37 rad central lock, then responds 10% faster and sheds 12% more lock as speed rises.

On the deterministic flat-road handling check, the Classic GTA profile reaches 38.9 m/s after eight seconds, peaks at 71.3 m/s over 60 seconds, stops from 25 m/s in 16.3 m, and turns 1.90 rad in a two-second 18 m/s corner. Those are simulation regression values, not brochure performance claims.

## Fit and integration boundary

The shape is approximately 4.5 m long, 1.76 m wide across the body, and 1.43 m high, with a 2.57 m wheelbase. Mirrors extend beyond the body width. Actual cooked bounds and counts are recorded in `cook_report.json`; current body count is 18,880 triangles, below the 19,000-triangle cap. Checks enforce clear door/window apertures, door-to-quarter steps below 4 mm, narrow backed center/roof seams, no fixed quarter covering the rear doors, and 52 tire steering poses. The current measured door steps are below 1 mm. A further 57 exterior depth/material samples verify the rear lamp faces, rims, roundel, script, plate lip, backing, and mounting plane against adjacent paint. The previous rear model fails these checks with over 82 mm of lamp stand-off. The optional `--door-sweep` report checks 280 horizontal section poses against the fixed shell and the adjacent closed door, with a 1 mm endpoint tolerance; this is a sampled motion check, not a full solid collision proof. The current model has no reported section crossings. Matched `doors`, `ajar`, and `open` renders were inspected, and Asset Lab completed 45 frames with zero GL errors.

Saved Blender axes are −X driver-left, +Y forward and +Z up. Runtime axes are +X driver-left, +Y up and +Z forward. Construction geometry and hinge values in `cook_report.json` use +X driver-left, +Y forward and +Z up; convert the hinge coordinates before runtime use.

The 360 is selectable under BWC → 360 and appears in moving and parked ambient traffic. The player rig uses the custom wheels, six transparent panes, left-side driver entry/exit, and the front passenger door control. Rear doors remain closed in the current game rig; all four authored hinges remain in Blender/GLB. `body_drive.emesh` contains the fixed shell and rear door skins; `body_traffic.emesh` contains all closed skins. Neither runtime variant bakes glass into opaque paint. Both versions use the authored randomized plate mounts. Traffic preserves the model's native dimensions and has a matching collision footprint.
